tuner

Drop a photograph in. An adaptive quadtree finds where the picture is hard to describe with one flat colour, and every cell it stops at becomes a single brush stroke. Stretched along the image’s own structure, mixed from a palette that can only reach the colours real tubes can, and laid down coarse to fine over an underpainting.

  • Adaptive quadtree stroke allocation
  • Structure-tensor orientation
  • Mixed-pigment palettes & painter’s flow fields

Colophon

Nothing is uploaded and nothing is fetched while you work. The pipeline is a JavaScript port of a small Python package, held to it stage by stage by a parity suite, and it runs in a worker so that a render of a few seconds cannot freeze the sliders. A large photograph at full res is genuinely tens of seconds of arithmetic on your own processor; the busy chip in the corner of the picture carries a clock so that slow can be told from stuck.

Built on

  • Haeberli, Paint By Numbers: Abstract Image Representations, SIGGRAPH Computer Graphics 24(4), 1990 — the founding idea, a painting as an ordered collection of strokes sampling the source.
  • Litwinowicz, Processing Images and Video for an Impressionist Effect, SIGGRAPH 97 — strokes oriented along the isophotes, and randomly perturbed to keep the hand in them.
  • Hertzmann, Painterly Rendering with Curved Brush Strokes of Multiple Sizes, SIGGRAPH 98, and Fast Paint Texture, NPAR 2002 — the opaque coarse underpainting, and the height field that makes a stroke edge a ridge.
  • Samet, The Quadtree and Related Hierarchical Data Structures, ACM Computing Surveys 16(2), 1984 — the subdivision and its variance criterion.
